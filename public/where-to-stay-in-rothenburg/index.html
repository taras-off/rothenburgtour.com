Where to Stay in Rothenburg: Old Town or Outside the Walls?

The real choice is inside the walls or outside them — and it turns on the car, not the room.

By Eugene · updated 12 October 2026

Rothenburg ob der Tauber is a walled oval about a kilometre long. You can cross it in twenty minutes. So the question is not which end of town to sleep in — nothing is more than a ten-minute walk from the centre — and it is not really about stars or breakfast either.

The question is whether you sleep inside the walls or outside them, and the honest answer depends on one thing most booking sites will not tell you: whether you are arriving by car.

What this page does not do is tell you the town's story. That is the TouringBee audio guide: 25 stops, about 90 minutes, offline, for the daylight hours in between.

What a night inside the walls actually buys

Not the room. The hours.

Coach groups arrive from about 10:30 and are gone by about 16:00. Between those times Rothenburg is about as busy as a town this size can get. After the shops close, the day visitors leave, the street lighting comes on and the lanes empty out — and the town that appears is the one the photographs are selling.

The same thing happens at the other end. At 07:30 the Plönlein is empty. By 10:30 it has a queue of people photographing it.

If you sleep inside the walls you get both of those for free, by walking downstairs. If you sleep outside them you still get them, by walking ten minutes. If you do not sleep here at all, you do not get them, and you will have seen Rothenburg at its worst hour and wondered what the fuss was about.

That is the whole argument, and it is why this site keeps making it.

The car problem, which is the real decision

Here is the part that actually separates inside from outside, and it is about parking rules rather than charm.

You can drive into the old town. There is no general daytime ban on cars, whatever you may have read — including on earlier versions of this site. What there is instead is a set of rules that make keeping a car inside the walls awkward and expensive.

Height limit. From 2.85 m up you cannot enter the old town without a HöhenVignette, and even with one the ceiling is 3.90 m. That rules out most campervans and some roof boxes.

Parking inside the walls is capped at two hours. Short-stay spaces cost €2.00 an hour, charged daily 08:00–20:00, with a two-hour maximum. That is fine for unloading. It is no use for a night.

So you need one of two things. Either a GästeVignette — a guest permit your hotel buys for your specific number plate, which lets you park free for that day — or a Tagesbesucherkarte, a visitor card valid 24 hours. Both are good for longer than two hours; that is the point of them. It costs €30 to keep the car inside the walls and €8 to leave it at the car parks outside. The guest permit is the one you want, and it is worth asking before you book: does the hotel issue a GästeVignette, and for inside or outside the walls?

Outside the walls, parking is simple. The P1–P5 car parks cost €1.30 an hour, capped at €8 for the day, charged 09:00–18:00 and free outside those hours. No time limit. The €8 outside-the-walls day card above is the same €8, bought in one go rather than by the hour. P1 sits by the Rödertor on the east side, P4 by the Galgentor, P5 to the north.

Arriving. Overnight guests load and unload through the Galgentor, next to P4. Your satnav probably will not know this, and may route you into a lane you cannot turn round in. Ask your hotel for the approach before you set off — not the address, the approach.

Roadworks matter more here than in most towns, because the lanes are single-file and a closure has no parallel route. The Georgengasse is shut for all of 2026, and the Spitalgasse at the Heilig-Geist-Kirche has been closed since 13 April 2026 and is expected to stay shut into mid-November.

So: inside, or outside?

Stay inside the walls if you are arriving by train, if you are staying two nights or more, if the evening is the reason you came, or if you want to be able to go back to the room for a coat. The premium is real but so is what it buys — see what is available inside the walls.

Stay outside the walls if you are driving and your hotel inside cannot sort out a permit, if you want a bigger room for the money, if you are travelling with a campervan, or if you simply want to park once and forget about it. The walk in takes about ten minutes and ends at a town gate, which is not the worst way to arrive.

Outside is not the consolation prize here that it would be in a bigger city. The town is too small for that, and the places just outside the gates are worth comparing before you pay the old-town premium.

Which part of the old town?

The old town is small enough that location barely affects walking time, but it does affect noise and access.

Around Marktplatz and Herrngasse is the centre of everything: closest to the restaurants, the clock, the tourist office and the Christmas market. It is also where the daytime crowd is thickest and where you will hear the church bells. Good for a short stay, less good for a light sleeper.

The Klingengasse and Judengasse side, to the north, is quieter, residential, and two minutes from the square. This is the compromise most people are actually looking for.

A room near the Rödertor, on the east, puts you next to a marked staircase onto the wall and next to P1, which is handy if you are driving in and out.

Down by the Spitalgasse, at the southern tip, is quieter and cheaper and about eight minutes from the square, with the Plönlein on your way in every morning. Check the roadworks note above before driving to this one.

Advent: what changes

The Reiterlesmarkt runs 20 November to 23 December 2026, closed on Totensonntag, 22 November. Three things change.

Rooms go early. A town this size with a market this well known rarely has spare capacity on an Advent Saturday. If you are coming for a market weekend, book it the way you would book a flight, not the way you would book a hotel — check what is still free for your dates.

The old town closes to cars for part of each market weekend. The walled town is shut to traffic on Saturdays 14:00–18:00 and Sundays 11:00–18:00, residents excepted. If you are driving to a hotel inside the walls on a market Saturday, arrive before 14:00 or plan to leave the car outside.

Staying inside is worth more in December than in July. The market closes at 19:00 or 20:00, the lanes stay lit, and the half hour after it shuts is the half hour the season is actually about. That half hour is only available to people who are not driving home.

If you are not staying, or not staying here

If you have one afternoon between Munich and Frankfurt and no intention of staying the night, this page cannot help you and neither can a hotel. Take the afternoon, accept that you are seeing the busy version, and come back properly another time. If you are doing it that way regardless, the travel is the part worth outsourcing: compare organised day trips from Munich, Frankfurt and Nuremberg.

And if Rothenburg is a stop on a longer Romantic Road drive, there is a reasonable case for sleeping in a cheaper town nearby and coming in for the evening. You lose the morning, which is the smaller of the two prizes.

While you are here, in daylight

The hours either side of the day trippers are the reason to stay. The hours in the middle are the ones where it helps to know what you are looking at.

Why does the wall have two rings? What happened here in 1945, and whose names are carved into the stones along it? What legend do the clock figures act out every hour? And who was Toppler?

The scripts are written by historians, which is why the answers are specific rather than the single line a guidebook gives you.

Practical notes

Parking figures come from the city's own fee regulation; roadworks and market dates from the city's own notices, all current for the 2026/27 season. Check before you travel — all three change.

Ask that one question before booking. The answer changes the real price of the room more than the nightly rate does. The city's own RothenburgPlusKarte takes the first hour off inside the walls and the first two hours at the car parks, but it does not lift the two-hour cap, so it is not an answer to this one.

Luggage. Medieval cobble, everywhere, including between the gate and your door. Wheeled cases do badly. If you are arriving on foot from a car park, pack for the walk.

Check-in times and the car. Short-stay parking inside the walls is capped at two hours. If you arrive before your room is ready you cannot simply leave the car there all afternoon.

Cash. Smaller guesthouses, and several of the sights, still prefer it.

Bells. The churches are working churches, and the old town is compact. A room facing a square is a room facing a bell tower.

Accessibility. Old buildings, narrow stairs, and no lifts in many of the smaller places. If steps are an issue, ask specifically rather than filtering — the listings are not always accurate about it.

In short

Sleep inside the walls if you can, because the reason to come to Rothenburg happens before the coaches arrive and after they leave, and you cannot day-trip to it. Sleep outside if you are driving and the permit question gets a vague answer — the walk is ten minutes and the evening is the same evening.

Either way, do not do it as a day trip and then tell people Rothenburg is a tourist trap. You will have been there for the only part that is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it worth staying inside the walls in Rothenburg?

For most visitors, yes — not for the room but for the hours. Day visitors arrive from about 10:30 and are gone by about 16:00, so the quiet town exists early and late. Staying outside the walls gets you the same hours with a ten-minute walk, for less money.

Can you drive into the old town in Rothenburg?

Yes. There is no general daytime ban on cars. The limits are a 2.85 m height restriction, a two-hour cap on short-stay parking inside the walls at €2.00 an hour, and weekend closures during the Christmas market. Overnight guests load and unload through the Galgentor.

Where do you park if your hotel is inside the walls?

Ask the hotel for a GästeVignette, a one-day guest permit issued to your number plate that allows free parking. Without one, a 24-hour visitor card costs €30 inside the old town or €8 outside, and the P1–P5 car parks outside the walls are €1.30 an hour to a daily maximum of €8.

How far is it from the car parks to the old town?

About ten minutes on foot from any of P1–P5, ending at one of the town gates. The whole walled town is roughly a kilometre long.

How far in advance should you book for the Christmas market?

Earlier than feels necessary, especially for Saturday nights between 20 November and 23 December 2026. Rothenburg is a small town with a famous market and limited beds.

Is there a train to Rothenburg?

Not a mainline one. You change at Steinach (b Rothenburg), about 15 km away, onto a short branch line with roughly hourly connections.

Should you stay in Rothenburg or visit as a day trip?

Stay, if the choice is yours. A day trip arrives and leaves inside the busiest window of the day, which is the version of Rothenburg that disappoints.
