Rothenburg Self-Guided Walking Tour: A Complete Route

Nine points, about ninety minutes, with a map. Where to go, what it costs and when — free.

By Eugene · updated 2 October 2026

Get the Audio Guide The Route Quick Answers

Rothenburg ob der Tauber is a walled oval you can cross in twenty minutes, and people still manage to spend a day here badly — doubling back, missing the wall, standing in the square at the hour when every coach in Bavaria is standing in it too.

The route below fixes that part. Ninety minutes on foot, nine points, no backtracking: the wall, the Plönlein, the market square, the castle garden and the quiet lanes, in an order that works. It is free, needs no booking, and you can start it at any hour.

What it does not do is tell you the town’s story — why the wall has two rings, what happened here in 1945, who Toppler was, where the local legends come from. For that there is the TouringBee audio guide: 25 stops, about 90 minutes, offline.

The route: nine points, about 90 minutes

1. Rödertor, the eastern gate. Start here. The P1 car park is just outside it and the station is about ten minutes’ walk away, and there is a marked staircase onto the wall right at the gate. Doing the wall first means doing it on fresh legs. Toilets are at the car park; there are none on the wall.

2. The wall, east to south. Up the stairs, turn right. You are on a covered wooden walkway — tiled roof overhead, arrow slits at eye level, and a view down into back gardens you never see from the street. About twenty minutes at a normal pace. The boards are uneven and the passage is narrow enough that two people pass sideways; in the wet it is slippery. No ticket, no gate.

Many of the stones carry carved names. They are worth looking at, and there is a reason for them that this page is not going to explain.

3. Spitalbastei. Walk through the southern bastion rather than past it: two inner courtyards and a sequence of gateways let you feel the thickness of the defences in a way the street never does. Five minutes, free. Approaching on foot from the south gives you the full depth of the gates.

4. Up Spitalgasse to the Plönlein. Ten minutes uphill on cobbles. The Plönlein is the fork with the yellow half-timbered house, a fountain and a tower on either side — the most recognisable view in Rothenburg, and in season there is usually a queue of people photographing it. The best chance of catching it near-empty is early, roughly 07:00 to 08:00; it also calms down in the evening.

5. Schmiedgasse to Marktplatz. Five minutes, slightly uphill. The market square has the town hall on one side and gabled merchant houses around it. On the Ratstrinkstube, figures come out of the clock and act out the Meistertrunk legend — the town’s own visitor material gives the window as 10 a.m. to 10 p.m., on the hour. It lasts about a minute and people stand in the middle of the square for it.

Optional: the Town Hall Tower. 220 steps to a platform at 52 metres and a view over the whole roofscape. Two things to know before you commit: the ticket desk is at the top of the climb, not the bottom, and it takes cash only — €4 adult, €2 under 14. The final section is a single-file hatch, so skip it if tight spaces are a problem. Hours: April–October daily 09:30–12:30 and 13:00–17:00; January–March and November, weekends only 12:00–15:00; during the Christmas market daily 10:30–14:00 and 14:30–18:00, Friday to Sunday until 19:00.

6. Herrngasse west to the Burggarten. Ten minutes, gently downhill, along the widest street in town and out through the Burgtor. The castle garden sits on the western tip of the ridge: free, open, and a wide view over the Tauber valley and the southern fortifications. If you would rather not climb the tower, this is the panorama to take instead — it is a different view, not a lesser one.

7. Optional: down into the valley. To see Rothenburg from outside its walls, take the path down from the garden toward the Topplerschlösschen and the double-arched bridge. The descent is 15–20 minutes; allow about 45 for the round trip, and remember it is uphill coming back.

8. Back east through the lanes. Rather than retracing Herrngasse, cut back through the small streets toward Judengasse. It is noticeably quieter than the main shopping streets and gives you a different side of the old town. Ten minutes to the square.

9. The north wall: Klingentor back to Rödertor. Rejoin the walkway on the north side. This stretch usually feels calmer than the central streets, and it is at its best in low evening light. From the gallery near the Röderbastei you look down on the Gerlachschmiede and its distinctive triangular roof — the view most people only get from street level. Twenty minutes, and you are back at the start.

The wall: how much of it to walk

The town’s own figures: the wall runs about 4 km around the old town with 42 towers, and the marked circuit along it — the Rothenburger Turmweg — takes roughly 2.5 hours. Large parts of the walkway are open around the clock and cost nothing.

The loop above uses two sections of it, about forty minutes in total, which is the amount most people enjoy. Doing the full 4 km in one go is a long stretch on uneven boards under a low roof, and it is the point at which a wall walk starts to feel like a corridor. If you want all of it, split it across two visits or save a section for the evening.

The town’s marked Tower Walk is described by the tourist office as almost barrier-free, which makes it the better option if stairs are a problem. The wall walkway itself is not: access is by staircase at the gates — Rödertor, Würzburger Tor and Galgentor among them — and there is no lift anywhere on it.

When to walk it

Early morning is the best chance to see the Plönlein and the market square before the day gets going, and the light is on your side.

The middle of the day is when the town is most alive — shops, museums and towers all open — and also when the central streets carry the most groups. Good hours for the things that need a ticket, less good for photographs.

Evening, once the daytime sights have closed, the central streets gradually empty and the lighting comes on. If you are staying the night, keep a section of wall and a few lanes for this part of the day. That is the argument for a bed inside the walls rather than a day trip.

Shoes matter more than the weather. The whole old town is medieval cobble and the walkway is uneven boards that get slippery when wet. Flat soles with some cushioning; heels are a bad idea here.

Practical notes

Prices and hours verified for the 2026/27 season against the town’s official tourism material. Check before you travel — they change.

Getting to the start. No mainline station; change at Steinach (b Rothenburg) for the shuttle. By car, the P1–P5 car parks sit outside the walls, and driving on most old-town streets is closed to visitors between 11:00 and 16:00. If you would rather come on an organised round trip, compare the day trips from Munich, Frankfurt and Nuremberg — most leave three to five free hours in town, which is this loop plus lunch.

Toilets. At the car parks and around the market square. None on the wall.

Food and water. The loop passes the market square, which is where the cafés are. Nothing is open on the walkway.

Rain. The walkway has a roof, which makes it the most comfortable thing in town in bad weather. The cobbles are the problem, not the sky.

Accessibility. The main streets and squares are reachable without steps, but the surface in the old town is mostly cobbled and can be hard going on small wheels. The wall walkway and both climbable towers are stairs only. The town’s marked Tower Walk is the accessible alternative.

In short

If time is short, do not try to walk the whole ring. Start at Rödertor, take the southern stretch of wall, come up through the Plönlein to the market square, carry on to the castle garden and return through the northern lanes. Ninety minutes to two hours, and you will have seen the spaces that make Rothenburg what it is, without walking anything twice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you walk Rothenburg’s city walls for free?

Yes. Large parts of the walkway are free and open around the clock. The town puts the wall at about 4 km with 42 towers, and its marked circuit, the Turmweg, at roughly 2.5 hours.

How long does it take to walk around Rothenburg?

The loop on this page takes about 90 minutes. The town’s own short tour is given as about 1.5 hours and its grand tour as about 2.5 hours, so the loop sits in the normal range.

Where should I start a walking tour of Rothenburg?

Rödertor, the eastern gate. There is a marked staircase onto the wall, the P1 car park is beside it, and starting there means you walk the wall while you are fresh and finish in the centre.

Is there a self-guided walking tour app for Rothenburg?

Yes — the TouringBee audio tour: 25 stops, about 90 minutes, fully offline with its own map, in eight languages, from €9.99 for the guide.

What is the best time of day to walk Rothenburg?

Early morning for quiet streets and good light; the middle of the day if you want things open; the evening once the daytime sights have shut and the lighting comes on.

Do I need good shoes?

Yes. The old town is medieval cobble and the wall walkway is uneven boards that are slippery in the wet. Flat, cushioned soles.

Is the walk suitable for wheelchairs or pushchairs?

The streets and squares are mostly step-free but cobbled, which is hard on small wheels. The wall walkway and both towers are stairs only; the town’s Tower Walk is described as almost barrier-free.
